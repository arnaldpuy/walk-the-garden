One question, 360 analytical pathways

Fifty Years of Forking Paths

Has half a century of research reduced the uncertainty in global irrigation water withdrawals? Puy and colleagues compile 1,624 global estimates from 65 studies published between 1974 and 2025 — and instead of answering the question down one analytical pathway, they walk all 360 defensible ones at once.

Why walk every pathway

Assessing whether uncertainty in irrigation water withdrawal (IWW) estimates has declined requires methodological choices with no single defensible answer: which target year to examine, how wide to bin studies over publication time, whether to admit estimates within an interval around the target year, whether bins overlap, and which statistic measures spread. Each choice is a fork, and their full factorial — 5 × 3 × 2 × 2 × 6 — opens 360 analytical pathways. Any single study would walk one of them and report its endpoint as the answer; the left panel walks the paper's first example pathway, where the spread of estimates for 2050 grows across study periods.

The paper instead runs the whole garden. Across all 360 pathways, only 17% show any reduction in uncertainty over time — falling to 10% once projections to 2100 are excluded — while 40% show an increase and 34% an unstable, non-monotonic trend. A random forest over the results identifies the choice of target year as the most influential methodological decision. The conclusion inverts the field's fifty-year goal: rather than chasing a narrower spread, research may need to deliberately expand uncertainty until it reflects the true breadth of plausible estimates.
